regenerated by `scripts/lqr_crossover/report.py`) against `docs/prereg_lqr_crossover.md`
as registered at `33c7632`, plus the three additions the close-out asked for: the
provenance gap, the RMS-convention consistency check, one post-hoc `d = 6` arm

## 3. Consistency with the RMS proposition

**Construction.** The LQR field is single-frequency: one `omega` shared by `r`
orthonormal directions, `e = (eps/sqrt r) sum_j sin(omega v_j^T a + phi_j)` — the same
form as the `J`-mode planted field. With `theta_j = omega v_j^T mu + phi_j`, the
population moments over `a ~ N(mu, sigma^2 I)` are closed form:

```
E[e^2]/eps^2         = (1/r)[ sum_j (1/2 - 1/2 e^{-2c^2} cos 2 theta_j) + sum_{i!=j} e^{-c^2} sin theta_i sin theta_j ]
E||grad e||^2/eps^2  = (omega^2/r) sum_j (1/2 + 1/2 e^{-2c^2} cos 2 theta_j)
omega_RMS^2 = E||grad e||^2 / E[e^2],     r_RMS = sigma omega_RMS / sqrt d
```

The sweep stores `phi` and `v^T mu` per state (`sweep.py`) but not the sampled `u`, so
these are the exact population values at the realised phases, not sample moments; they
are the quantities the `s3` statistics estimate against. `omega_RMS -> omega` as
`c -> inf` for every rank; at the crossover cells it is already there:
`omega_RMS/omega` = 1.0000, 1.0034, 1.0001, 1.0000, 1.0000, 1.0000, 1.0000, 1.0000 at
`d` = 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 16, 32, 64 (`lqr_audit_rms.csv`).

**The product `(Var_e[ZO]/Var_e[PW]) * r_RMS^2`**, `M = 32`, cells with
`r_nom ∈ [0.5, 3]` (`lqr_audit_rms.csv`):

| arm | d = 1 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rank-one, median | 1.438 | 1.142 | 1.037 | 1.034 | 1.033 | 1.032 | 1.033 | 1.032 |
| rank-one, min–max | 1.04–2.44 | 1.03–1.71 | 1.03–1.15 | 1.03–1.14 | 1.03–1.05 | 1.03–1.04 | 1.03–1.04 | 1.03–1.04 |
| r = 2, median | 1.446 | 1.108 | 1.037 | — | 1.036 | 1.034 | 1.033 | 1.033 |
| full rank, median | 1.440 | 1.123 | 1.041 | — | 1.032 | 1.032 | 1.032 | 1.033 |

**Predicted constant, and why it is not the appendix's.** The prereg mandates the
`M/(M-1)` de-attenuation on `g_ZO` (§2; `sweep.py` applies it). That multiplies
`Var_e[ZO]` by `(M/(M-1))^2`, so the leading-order product is

```
this study (de-attenuated ZO):  M/(M-1)   = 1.03226   ->  r*_RMS = sqrt(M/(M-1)) = 1.0160
planted sweep (plain centred ZO): (M-1)/M = 0.96875   ->  r*_RMS = sqrt(1 - 1/M)  = 0.9843
```

The measured medians are 1.032–1.037 for `d >= 4` at every rank, i.e. `M/(M-1)` to
0.5%. The appendix's `r_RMS* = sqrt(1 - 1/M)` is correct **for the estimator the planted
sweep used**; for the estimator this study registered it must read `sqrt(M/(M-1))`, and
`reference.py`'s asymptote `c* -> sqrt(d M/(M-1))` is that statement. Same derivation,
one factor of `(M/(M-1))^2` apart; the appendix should say which estimator it means.

**Small `d`.** Medians rise to 1.11–1.14 at `d = 2` and 1.44 at `d = 1`: the along-`v`
term `Lambda` of `reference.py` is not `Vsin/(M-1)`, and its share is `1/d`. This is the
deviation the leading-order formula predicts, largest at `d = 1`, gone by `d = 8`.

**Are 0.4876 / 0.4870 / 0.4871 the RMS relabelling?** Yes. `omega_RMS = omega` at every
crossover cell to four digits, so `r_RMS = r_nom = sigma omega/sqrt d` there, and the
nominal-`omega` exponents **are** the RMS-convention exponents at every rank. The
`-0.0129` is the sup-norm (`omega/sqrt r`) relabelling of the same fit — the reading that
moves by `sqrt r`, as it did by `sqrt J` in the planted study.

## 7. Reviewer assessment

1. **What the study establishes.** In a closed-form LQR with a planted single-frequency
   error field, the production estimator core reproduces the error-channel identities of
   `reference.py` to Monte Carlo precision (`c*` within 1.8 SE at every registered `d`),
   the crossover collapses onto `sigma*omega` (column sd ≤ 0.008), and the fitted
   exponent is 0.4876 [0.4869, 0.4883] on the registered `d`-set — a verification of the
   rank-one algebra, as the prereg says in advance, not a test of Claim 4.
2. **What the registered rules actually return.** Rule A: VERIFIED, with the 4-SE
   clause passing only under the exact-aggregation closed form (the report's own column
   would fail it at `d = 2, 4`). Rule B: REFUTED at full rank (`-0.0129`, an arithmetic
   shift of 0.4871), on a three-rung ladder where four were registered and one rung has
   a CI. Cross term: the registered rule flags every `d`; the report's variant flags
   {1, 2, 4} and now 6.
3. **Does `sqrt d` at fixed rank survive the RMS reading?** Yes, exactly: `omega_RMS =
   omega` at every crossover cell, so 0.4876/0.4870 are already RMS-convention exponents,
   and the product `(Var_ZO/Var_PW) r_RMS^2` sits at `M/(M-1) = 1.032` for `d >= 4` at
   all three ranks. The appendix's constant `sqrt(1 - 1/M)` belongs to the un-de-attenuated
   estimator; this study's is `sqrt(M/(M-1))`.
4. **Is `d = 6` inside the cross-term flag set?** Inside under the at-crossover rule
   (ZO 0.857, PW 0.330), inside under the registered all-grid rule (as is everything),
   outside under the bounded total-MSE reading (0.057 / 0.012).
5. **Strongest remaining objection.** The one result that could have failed — Rule B —
   is adjudicated on a ladder that is missing its `ceil(sqrt d)` rung and its per-rank
   CIs, with the sign-flip table printing `nan`; and the whole study is one planted
   frequency in a quadratic `Q^pi`, so "which regime describes a real critic" (E3)
   remains the load-bearing, untested question the report itself names.
